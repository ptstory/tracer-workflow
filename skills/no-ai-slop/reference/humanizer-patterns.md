# Humanizer pattern reference

Derived from `blader/humanizer` `SKILL.md` and `README.md` at commit
`e2e92e7b4b8229253ed5c8e81dc65463fdeddda5` (Humanizer v2.11.2).
Use this file only as a supplemental findings checklist for
`skills/no-ai-slop/SKILL.md`. It is not a ban list.

Non-bans in `skills/no-ai-slop/SKILL.md`: em dashes, emojis, title case,
bold-label lists, passive voice, groups of three, repeated sentence openings,
and rhetorical fragments.

## Content patterns

| # | Pattern | Audit note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Inflated importance and legacy | Ordinary facts are made historic or pivotal. |
| 2 | Name-dropping to prove importance | Well-known names are listed without useful context. |
| 3 | Shallow -ing analysis | Trailing -ing clauses pretend to explain meaning. |
| 4 | Sales language | Descriptions read like ads instead of facts. |
| 5 | Vague sources | Claims lean on unnamed experts or reports. |
| 6 | Formulaic challenges and outlook | Stock challenge/future paragraphs add no facts. |

## Language and grammar patterns

| # | Pattern | Audit note |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | Overused AI words | AI-favorite filler words pile up. |
| 8 | Avoiding is and are | Long verb phrases replace simple verbs. |
| 9 | Not X but Y and clipped endings | Binary contrast and clipped endings hide the point. |
| 10 | Forced groups of three | The draft forces triads that the meaning does not need. |
| 11 | Changing names and repeated openings | Same thing gets renamed or re-started too often. |
| 12 | False from X to Y ranges | Fake ranges replace the real topics. |
| 13 | Passive voice and missing subjects | Actors disappear when clarity needs them. |

## Style patterns

| # | Pattern | Audit note |
|---|---|---|
| 14 | Em and en dashes | Decorative dash rhythm can be overused. |
| 15 | Too much bold text | Bold becomes decoration instead of emphasis. |
| 16 | Lists with bold mini-headings | Lists replace prose where prose would be cleaner. |
| 17 | Title case in headings | Headings are capitalized like ad copy. |
| 18 | Emojis | Emojis decorate copy that does not need them. |
| 19 | Curly quotation marks | Curly quotes may clash with the target format. |

## Chatbot patterns

| # | Pattern | Audit note |
|---|---|---|
| 20 | Chatbot text left in the answer | The assistant's closing lines leak into the draft. |
| 21 | Knowledge-limit disclaimers and guesses | The draft guesses where it should state a limit. |
| 22 | Overly agreeable tone | The draft praises or agrees before answering. |

## Filler and hedging

| # | Pattern | Audit note |
|---|---|---|
| 23 | Filler phrases | Phrases delay the point without adding meaning. |
| 24 | Too many qualifiers | One caveat becomes three. |
| 25 | Generic positive endings | The draft ends on a vague send-off. |

## Other patterns

| # | Pattern | Audit note |
|---|---|---|
| 26 | Too many hyphenated word pairs | Hyphenation becomes a habit instead of grammar. |
| 27 | Pretending to reveal a deeper truth | The draft announces depth instead of showing it. |
| 28 | Announcing the next point | The draft narrates the structure instead of starting. |
| 29 | A heading repeated in the first sentence | The paragraph restates the heading. |
| 30 | Writing about the previous version | The draft talks about the old version instead of the current one. |
| 31 | Forced punchlines and dramatic fragments | Several fragments in a row feel staged. |
| 32 | Formulaic sayings | A proverb replaces the specific claim. |
| 33 | Fake-candid openings | "Honestly?" and similar hooks delay the answer. |
| 34 | Answering objections no one raised | The draft defends against a problem the text never named. |
| 35 | Rejecting fake alternatives | The draft invents an option only to dismiss it. |
